Soma Bloom and platform documents

Select a title to read the document on this page.

Soma Technologies is introducing Soma Bloom, a new artificial intelligence model designed specifically around research, education, students, and the realities of Sub-Saharan Africa.

Bloom is built around a simple idea: an AI system should not only know the world; it should understand the people and environments it is built to serve.

While general-purpose AI systems have made significant progress in research, education, and information retrieval, many remain primarily trained and optimized around datasets, languages, institutions, and educational environments outside Africa. Soma Bloom takes a different approach.

Bloom has been developed with a strong focus on African educational data, local languages, institutions, students, teachers, and the specific challenges faced by education systems across the continent.

What is Soma Bloom?

Soma Bloom is Soma Technologies' research-focused AI model and the intelligence layer of the wider Soma ecosystem.

Its primary purpose is to help students, teachers, researchers, and educational institutions find information, understand difficult concepts, conduct research, learn independently, and solve problems more effectively.

Bloom is designed to function as more than a chatbot. It is intended to become an AI research and education infrastructure layer for Africa.

The model is particularly focused on:

  • Academic research and information synthesis
  • Student learning and tutoring
  • Educational explanations
  • Research assistance
  • African and Zimbabwean educational contexts
  • Local languages and terminology
  • Understanding local institutions and environments
  • Supporting teachers and reducing the teacher-to-student gap
  • Helping students learn independently
  • Developing AI solutions relevant to African communities

Built around African educational data

A central part of Bloom's development has been its exposure to local educational information.

The model has been developed using data and research relating to approximately 13,000 Zimbabwean schools, alongside additional work involving local organizations, startups, researchers, universities, and government institutions.

This gives Bloom a foundation that is substantially different from that of a general-purpose international AI model.

The objective is not simply to make Bloom "more African" as a branding exercise. The objective is to make it more useful when the question itself is African.

A student researching Zimbabwean education, a teacher working within a Zimbabwean school, a researcher studying Sub-Saharan African education, or a developer building a local educational solution should not have to translate their environment into assumptions designed for another part of the world.

Bloom is being built to understand that environment from the beginning.

Research performance

Before its release, Soma Bloom has undergone internal evaluation against contemporary AI systems, including research-oriented systems such as Perplexity and selected GPT and other leading models.

In these evaluations, Bloom has demonstrated performance broadly within the same range as several established systems on relevant research and educational tasks, while remaining behind some of the strongest current frontier models in certain capabilities.

Bloom is not being presented as the most capable general-purpose AI model in the world. Some frontier systems currently maintain advantages in areas such as advanced reasoning, complex coding, multimodal capabilities, and general intelligence.

Instead, Bloom's objective is different:

Build the most useful research and education AI for Africa.

Performance is therefore evaluated not only through generic benchmarks, but also through its usefulness in the environments it was designed for.

Local knowledge and languages

One of Bloom's major areas of focus is local knowledge.

The model has been initially developed around African educational environments, including Zimbabwean terminology, institutions, curriculum-related information, and local linguistic contexts.

This provides an opportunity to build capabilities around African languages and educational contexts that are frequently underrepresented in mainstream AI systems.

Bloom's long-term objective is to expand this foundation beyond Zimbabwe and develop stronger capabilities across Sub-Saharan Africa.

Bridging the teacher gap

One of Bloom's most important goals is educational access.

Across many parts of Sub-Saharan Africa, students can face significant limitations in access to teachers, specialized subjects, research resources, and individualized academic support.

AI cannot replace teachers. Instead, Bloom is designed around the idea that AI can extend the reach of teachers.

A teacher should be able to use Bloom to prepare material, explain concepts, research topics, and support students. A student should be able to use Bloom when they need an explanation, research assistance, or additional learning support outside the classroom.

The goal is not to remove the teacher from education. The goal is to give every teacher and student access to an additional layer of intelligence.

A collaborative African project

Soma Bloom is being developed as a collaborative project rather than the work of a single individual or institution.

The project includes developers and contributors from Zimbabwe and Botswana, with involvement and collaboration connected to universities and educational communities including:

  • African University
  • National University of Science and Technology (NUST)
  • University of Zimbabwe (UZ)
  • Chinhoyi University of Technology (CUT)
  • Women's University in Africa (WUA)

Additional collaboration and supporting relationships involve researchers, local startups, government institutions, and other organizations.

The project is intentionally open to further technical and research collaboration. Researchers, developers, academics, and institutions interested in improving Bloom are invited to contribute to its development and enhancement.

Designed by Soma Technologies

The product and visual design direction for Soma Bloom was created by Tanatswa Chiyangwa, founder of Soma Technologies.

Bloom is part of Soma Technologies' broader vision of building technology around the needs of African education systems.

Rather than treating education as another application of generic AI, Soma is building toward an ecosystem in which AI, educational software, research tools, student platforms, and institutional infrastructure work together. Bloom represents the intelligence layer behind that vision.

The long-term vision

Version 1.0.0 is only the beginning.

The long-term objective of Soma Bloom is to develop an AI system capable of understanding increasingly large portions of the African educational landscape while becoming more useful to students, teachers, researchers, institutions, and governments.

Future development is expected to focus on:

  • Education: deeper curriculum understanding, tutoring, teacher assistance, and personalized learning.
  • Research: stronger academic search, synthesis, citations, literature analysis, and research workflows.
  • African languages: improved understanding and generation across African languages.
  • Local knowledge: deeper understanding of African institutions, communities, history, culture, economies, and educational systems.
  • Institutional AI: tools for schools, universities, governments, and educational organizations.
  • Open collaboration: enabling researchers and developers to contribute to improving the model.

Version 1.0.0

Soma Bloom 1.0.0 will be released on 1 November 2026.

This release marks the beginning of Soma Technologies' effort to build an AI system specifically optimized for research and education in Africa.

Bloom does not claim that Africa needs an AI that simply imitates the world's largest models. It is built around a different question:

What would an AI look like if it were built from the beginning to understand African students, African teachers, African researchers, and African educational systems?

Soma Bloom is our attempt to answer that question.

Soma Bloom 1.0.0. Research. Learn. Understand. Bloom.

1. Overview

Soma is an artificial intelligence and education technology ecosystem designed to provide a unified digital infrastructure for students, teachers, parents, schools, universities, researchers, and educational institutions.

At the center of the ecosystem is Soma Bloom, Soma's research and education AI model.

Rather than treating Bloom as an isolated chatbot, Soma is designed as a layered system in which artificial intelligence, educational data, institutional software, research infrastructure, and user applications operate together.

The architecture can be understood as five major layers:

User Applications → Soma Intelligence Layer → Knowledge & Data Layer → Institutional Systems → Infrastructure & Security

This architecture allows different Soma products to share intelligence while maintaining separation between users, institutions, and sensitive information.

3. The Soma intelligence layer

The intelligence layer is the central component connecting Soma applications to artificial intelligence. At its core is Soma Bloom 1.0.0.

Bloom is responsible for understanding user requests, reasoning over information, generating responses, assisting with research, explaining educational concepts, and interacting with the knowledge available to the Soma ecosystem.

However, Soma's architecture does not require every request to be answered directly from the model's internal parameters. Instead, the system can combine model intelligence with external information. This creates a broader architecture:

User Question
      │
      ▼
Request Understanding
      │
      ▼
Context Identification
      │
      ├───────────────┐
      │               │
      ▼               ▼
Soma Bloom       Knowledge Retrieval
      │               │
      │          ┌────┴────┐
      │          │         │
      │       Local Data  Web/Research
      │          │         │
      └──────────┴─────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
          Context Assembly
                 │
                 ▼
             Bloom
                 │
                 ▼
          Verified Response

This allows Soma to distinguish between knowledge already understood by the model and information that needs to be retrieved or verified.

4. Soma Bloom

Soma Bloom is the primary AI model within the Soma ecosystem. Bloom is designed around several major capabilities.

Research

Bloom is designed to assist users in discovering, interpreting, comparing, and synthesizing information. Research workflows can include:

  • Question decomposition
  • Information retrieval
  • Source analysis
  • Summarization
  • Comparison
  • Evidence synthesis
  • Academic explanation
  • Research assistance
Education

Bloom is designed to understand educational questions and provide explanations appropriate to the student's context. Rather than simply returning an answer, the system can be used to explain concepts, provide examples, simplify difficult material, and support independent learning.

Local intelligence

Bloom's initial development emphasizes Zimbabwean and African educational environments. This includes local educational terminology, institutions, curriculum-related information, languages, and contextual knowledge.

Personalization

Soma applications can provide Bloom with controlled context about the user. For example:

Student
   │
   ├── School
   ├── Grade
   ├── Subjects
   ├── Academic History
   ├── Learning Activity
   └── Preferences
          │
          ▼
       Bloom
          │
          ▼
  Personalized Response

The objective is to make the AI understand the learner without exposing information that the model or user is not authorized to access.

6. Educational knowledge graph

The long-term Soma architecture includes a structured representation of African educational knowledge. The system can connect entities such as:

Student
   │
   ├── attends → School
   │                 │
   │                 ├── teaches → Teacher
   │                 ├── offers → Subject
   │                 └── follows → Curriculum
   │
   ├── studies → Subject
   │

This structure allows Soma to reason about educational relationships rather than treating every piece of information as an isolated document.

For example, an educational AI system can understand that a student's question is associated with a particular subject, grade, curriculum, school environment, and potentially previous learning activity.

10. Soma products

The architecture is intentionally modular. Different applications provide different interfaces to the same underlying ecosystem.

Soma X

A productivity and AI environment for high-school students. It combines AI, research, documents, tasks, files, web access, and productivity tools.

Soma Z

An education platform designed for younger learners and primary schools.

Soma Connect

The communication and engagement layer connecting schools, teachers, parents, and students.

Soma Browser

A browser environment designed around AI-assisted research and education.

Soma Y

A planned university-oriented platform designed around higher education workflows.

Soma Admin

Institutional administration infrastructure.

Soma Finance

A transaction-focused financial layer for transactions conducted through the Soma ecosystem.

The products remain modular while sharing common authentication, APIs, data infrastructure, and intelligence services.

11. Identity and authentication

A central identity system is important because Soma serves multiple types of users. The architecture can distinguish between:

User
 │
 ├── Student
 ├── Teacher
 ├── Parent
 ├── Administrator
 ├── Researcher
 └── Institution

Authentication establishes identity. Authorization determines what that identity is allowed to access. This distinction is fundamental to the Soma architecture.

12. Privacy and data isolation

Soma is designed around controlled access to educational information. Sensitive institutional information should not automatically become part of the general model's knowledge. Instead:

General AI Knowledge
        +
Authorized Institutional Context
        +
Current Research Context
        │
        ▼
   Soma Response

The architecture therefore separates model knowledge from retrieved information, private institutional information, and individual user context. This separation is critical when deploying AI inside schools and educational institutions.

17. Security architecture

Security is treated as a system-level requirement rather than an additional feature. Important components include:

  • Authentication
  • Authorization
  • Role-based access control
  • API authentication
  • Database security
  • Data isolation
  • Secure storage
  • Logging
  • Monitoring
  • Rate limiting
  • Model access controls

The system should follow the principle:

Users receive the minimum data required to perform the task they are authorized to perform.

This becomes particularly important when deploying Soma within schools and government or institutional environments.

18. Scalability

The architecture is designed to allow Soma to expand from individual users to institutions and eventually larger regional deployments. The system can scale horizontally across:

Users
  ↓
Applications
  ↓
API Services
  ↓

  ↓
Databases / Storage

As adoption increases, individual services can be scaled independently. For example, research traffic, school-management traffic, authentication traffic, and AI inference traffic do not necessarily need to operate on the same infrastructure.

19. The African AI infrastructure vision

Soma's long-term architecture extends beyond a collection of applications. The objective is to develop an integrated African technology stack consisting of:

  • Applications for students, teachers, parents, schools, universities, and institutions.
  • Intelligence through Soma Bloom.
  • Knowledge through structured African educational and research data.
  • Infrastructure through APIs, databases, storage, authentication, and model-serving systems.
  • Community through researchers, developers, universities, startups, and institutions.

20. Future architecture

Soma's architecture is expected to evolve significantly as Bloom and the wider ecosystem mature. Future areas may include:

  • Multimodal AI
  • Voice-based education
  • African-language models
  • Advanced educational agents
  • AI research agents
  • Institutional knowledge graphs
  • Personalized learning systems
  • University infrastructure
  • Government education systems
  • Offline and low-connectivity AI
  • On-device AI
  • Regional African knowledge infrastructure

The objective is not to build one application. The objective is to build an interconnected intelligence infrastructure for education in Africa.

Conclusion

Soma is designed as an ecosystem rather than a standalone AI product. At its center is Soma Bloom, but Bloom is only one layer of a larger architecture combining artificial intelligence, educational data, institutional software, research infrastructure, and African developer and research communities.

The architecture is designed around one principle:

AI should understand the environment in which it is being used.

For Soma, that environment is Africa.

Soma Bloom 1.0.0 represents the first major release of this intelligence layer. The subsequent development of the Soma ecosystem will focus on increasing model capability, expanding African knowledge coverage, improving educational personalization, strengthening research capabilities, and enabling more researchers and institutions to participate in building the system.
